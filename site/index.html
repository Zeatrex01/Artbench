The art side of your game, in one place.

Artbench reads your Unity project, and the art folder you keep beside it: what each asset is, what uses it, what nothing uses, and what changed since you last looked. It runs on your computer.

The questions it answers

  • Which of these textures does nothing use?
  • What uses this material, and which scene does it end up in?
  • What changed after that pull?
  • Is the model in the game the one on my desktop?

Know what the game has, and what uses it

Every model, texture, material, prefab, scene, animation and sound of a Unity project, with previews, triangle counts and texture sizes. It reads the project's own files; Unity doesn't have to be running. What each asset uses, and what uses it, both ways.

Find what nothing uses

Assets that nothing in the project references get their own list, apart from the ones code loads by name. Check before you delete; Artbench never deletes anything itself.

See what changed since you last looked

After a pull, a branch switch or a teammate's work: what was added, changed, moved or removed, in one list. Mark it as seen and the list starts again from there.

Keep your art folder and the game in step

Keep your FBX, Blender and texture files in your own folder. Send a model to Unity, pull one back, or bring a Unity model into your folder: always with a plan you confirm first. The .meta file stays, the replaced copy is archived, and nothing is overwritten silently.

Notes that sit on the model, in Blender

The Artbench add-on pins notes and to-dos to your objects in Blender's 3D view. They're kept inside the .blend and work without Artbench. When it's running, one click sends them to its task board.

One task board, for you and your AI tool

Tasks with priorities, tied to an asset when you want. Claude Code, Gemini CLI and Codex can use Artbench too, over MCP: "What does nothing use?" or "Add a task to fix the sword grip." The Projects screen adds it to Claude Code in one click.

Nothing is overwritten silently

  • Artbench writes nothing into your Unity project on its own.
  • Every send or pull shows a plan, file by file, and waits for your OK.
  • A file changed on both sides is a conflict that waits for your decision.
  • Replaced copies go to an archive. Nothing is deleted.

Install

  1. Download Artbench {{VERSION}} for Windows and unzip it anywhere, but not inside a game project.
  2. Double-click Artbench.bat. If Windows SmartScreen warns you, choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Open Projects and pick your Unity project, and your art folder if you have one.

Optional

Blender 4.2 or newer adds model previews, triangle counts and what changed inside a model.

The Blender add-on is in the download (blender_addon\artbench_bridge.zip), or on its own. In Blender: Edit › Preferences › Get Extensions › Install from Disk.

Good to know

Usage and references need the Unity project saved as text (Unity's default, Force Text).

Settings stay in the Artbench folder. To remove it, delete the folder; nothing else is installed.
